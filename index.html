How the budget is spent
The finder screens every upgrade on a handful of seeds, then keeps halving: the better half is measured again on twice as many fresh seeds each round, until three finalists remain.
For a sharper answer
  • More time. A 1 h or 2 h limit runs more rounds, so the finalists are measured on many more seeds.
  • Fewer candidates. Search one player instead of the whole party, and try +1 item levels only: the same time is spread over fewer upgrades.
  • Longer runs. More hours per seed makes each measurement steadier, at the cost of fewer of them in the time limit.
  • The right fight. Pick the zone and tier you actually farm, with your real shrine, account buffs and pricing: the ranking is only as good as what it measures.

What this is

Paste one to five character exports, queue the zones and tiers you are curious about, and the simulator runs the whole party through each of them — rates, kills, deaths, XP and profit per day, plus a per-player breakdown of where every coin and every point of XP came from.
It runs entirely in this browser. The kernel is WebAssembly in Web Workers, one lane per core but one, so a queue of candidates goes as wide as your machine allows. Nothing you paste is uploaded — there is no server to upload it to.

Getting your export

Install MWI Party Export, open each party member's profile in-game once, then copy the export and paste it into Setup → Character exports. The whole party comes across in one paste: equipment, abilities and their triggers, levels, house rooms, consumables and the guild shrine, plus each player's owned gear and their alchemy and enhancing setup, which the Optimize tab uses to price upgrades.
A single character from another simulator's export works too, pasted the same way. Account-wide buffs (community buffs, Moo Pass, Wisdom Scroll) are not in any export, so those are fields you set once and this browser remembers.

Accuracy, and what is approximated

A run is a full combat simulation, not a formula: same seed, same inputs, same numbers. Market prices come from the game's public feed and refresh hourly. Checked against recorded play:
  • Pace, from three independent readings of the same party on Pirate Cove T2: a 27.7 h recording of 118 attempts averaged 13:57 an attempt, 8 separately timed runs averaged 13:52, and 10 complete wipe-free runs timed from the game's own wave messages averaged 14:02 ± 10 s. The kernel reads 13:54 over 5,723 simulated runs — inside all three, and 0.8 sigma from the tightest of them.
  • Casts per hour within 0.6% on every ability of the recording player over a 1,184-battle capture, read from the game's own cooldown messages.
  • XP over the same 27.7 h recording: 1.132M an hour in game against 1.134M simulated, within 0.2%.
  • Party totals against KikiMeter: damage −0.8%, healing −0.4%.
  • A solo open zone (Golem Cave T2, 210.9 h of combat): battles +0.35%, XP +0.61%, coin +0.46% and essence +1.04% above the game.
  • Run-to-run spread: 10 runs timed in game vary by a standard deviation of 32 ± 8 s against the simulated 27 s, and 13% of 10-run samples drawn from the kernel scatter at least that widely. No difference the data can see.
  • Armour and resistances: 20 of 20 values match five in-game Stats panels; a T2 Golem's whole stat block matches on 68 of 68 figures.
Where it approximates
  • Wave transition: a measured 12-bin table (3,023–3,090 ms, mean 3,036.9 ms) over the server's 120 s clock cycle, not a flat 3 s. Checked against 2,395 dungeon transitions of our own: the same pattern, bin for bin. Two later readings, timed from the last monster reaching 0 HP over 144 and then 695 transitions, came out 25 and 28 ms above the table — the message latency the next sentence describes. A reading from your own client sits above it by however long the wave message took to reach you, which moves what you see and not what the game does, so the field is for the server's gap and is best left empty. Runs use a fixed clock anchor, so the same inputs give the same numbers.
  • Server lag defaults to 0; if set, it is one constant delay on every action. An 8.8 h busy-hours capture found no per-action lag in any hour.
  • Dungeon wipe restart is a flat 3.0 s; an open-zone death is a flat 150 s respawn at full HP and MP (both measured).
  • Damage-over-time (3 s), regen (10 s), food/drink over-time (5 s) and enrage (60 s) ticks use the reference simulator's constants, not measured separately.
  • Dungeon waves draw from each lower wave table 1/8 of the time, fitted on 2,192 Pirate Cove T2 waves and applied to every dungeon.
  • Avg run is per CLEAR: the whole session divided by the runs that finished, so every failed attempt's time is charged to them. Trackers differ on this — MWI Insight averages per attempt, Toolasha prints both — so the per-attempt figure is in the column's tooltip to compare against directly. With a 1% fail rate the two sit about 9 s apart.
  • Loot, drops and chests are expected values, not rolls: a run's luck shows in kills, clears and deaths, never in item counts. Every party member gets an equal share of the mean.
  • Party level-gap penalty: the game's "20% below" is read as (top level ÷ yours) − 1, one of two possible readings, with the game's 10-level floor.
  • Every run starts at full mana, so very short runs read a little high. Compare runs of the same length.
  • Life steal and mana leech round down; neither has been tested against the game.
  • Healing is credited to the player who receives it; KikiMeter credits the caster, so compare party totals.
  • Thinly checked: how often a run fails, which is also what sets the Avg run figure. 1.14% of Pirate Cove T2 attempts wipe here against 2 of 118 recorded in game (1.69%), and none at all in a later 10-run stretch — the same number within the error two failures carry, which is about a percentage point either way. It matters because Avg run divides by CLEARS: per attempt the kernel and that recording agree to 0.3%, per clear they differ by 0.8%, and the whole of that 0.8% is the fail rate.
  • Deaths per hour are not validated: real sessions are too short to measure them well. A 30-minute capture's only deaths were three on one boss wave, against about 0.25 a run here — two boss fights is not evidence either way.
Where it is truer than the simulator it came from
  • The Task Badge counts: its Task Damage applies against the task monster you name, as in the game. The source simulators leave the badge out.
  • A stun, blind or silence runs its full length even when the monster that cast it dies first.
  • A wave spawning cancels whatever spell was mid-cast and starts a fresh one, on planets and in dungeons alike — watched in game, and confirmed in a capture: damage lands after the new cast begins in 2,083 of 2,093 player-waves. Auto-attack rhythm carries across the transition; new monsters start fresh. The source simulators carry the cast through.
  • The wave transition is a measured table, not a flat 3.000 s.
  • Dungeon waves sometimes draw from lower wave tables, which removes the source's dungeon XP overestimate: 201.8 monsters per run against 201.9–203.4 in captures.
  • Life Drain heals round up (15 of 15 captured casts).
  • Fury adds into the same damage and accuracy pool as other buffs instead of multiplying on top (12 of 12 Stats-panel readings).
  • The party level-gap penalty requires the game's 10-level difference.
  • Dungeon completion chests are modelled at all: 29.5% extra chests against 30.4% over a month of the game developer's data.
  • Fixed here: a player who died while stunned no longer comes back stunned at the dungeon clear, and a wave cleared by the swing that killed the last player restarts the dungeon instead of stalling it.

Found something wrong?

Mail Metzli in-game. Numbers that look off are worth reporting with the export and the zone you ran — a seed makes it reproducible, and reproducible is fixable.
